Beyrut'ta Tabbara ailesi, Lübnan'ın kuruluşundan bu yana yüz yılı aşkın süredir falafel satışı yapıyor. İki dünya savaşı ve çok sayıda yerel çatışmayı atlatan işletme, dördüncü nesil tarafından devralınmaya hazırlanıyor. 82 yaşındaki Muhammed Tabbara, 1914 yılında büyükbabası Kamel Tabbara tarafından Bab İdriss bölgesinde açılan dükkanda 6 yaşından beri çalıştığını belirtiyor. Century-Old Beirut Family Keeps Falafel Shop Alive Despite Two World Wars

ZBS deskNaharnet12 November 2026, 11:23
WhatsAppTelegramX

In Beirut, the Tabbara family has been selling falafel for over a hundred years since the founding of Lebanon. Surpassing two world wars and numerous local conflicts, the business is preparing to be taken over by the fourth generation. 82-year-old Mohammad Tabbara stated that he has been working since the age of 6 in the shop opened in 1914 by his grandfather Kamel Tabbara in the Bab Idriss area. Today, falafel sandwiches are sold for 3 dollars at the small shop operating in the Hamra district. His 40-year-old son, former accountant Ziad Tabbara, has been working full-time with his father for the last six years to continue this family legacy. The business provides an affordable meal in a country experiencing a deep economic crisis since 2019.
