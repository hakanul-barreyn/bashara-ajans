The silence index

From 7 October 2023 to today

An event may have reached the press, but who carried it? For every event we found in the headlines, we checked whether each outlet publishing at the time ran a single headline of that kind. In the first three months of the war, on average 84% of Western mainstream outlets carried no headline at all about an event; over the last six months, 94%. In the regional press the same measure was 68% and 76%.

Silence month by month, each event a dot

For every event we asked: what share of the outlets publishing at the time ran no headline about it at all? The chart shows this “silence rate”; higher means more silence. Each dot is one event; the lines are monthly averages. Hover a dot to see the event.

Denominator: every outlet publishing at the time. The figures we published until November 2026 use this measure. Outlets that barely follow Gaza also count as silent, so the rate runs high, especially for small events.

Method and limits

Events are found from the press: an event is a day on which one type of event (hospital, tent, people waiting for aid, mosque, school …) rises to two and a half times the median of the previous four weeks in the regional press. The index therefore counts only events that already reached the press; it does not see attacks that never became news (such as daily torture in detention). A comparison with real-world incident records (UN, the Ministry of Health, human rights groups) will be added as a separate chapter.

Counting is by type: an outlet that wrote about another event of the same type in those days counts as having covered it. The real silence is larger than shown.

The two measures answer two different questions. The original asks “what share of outlets publishing at the time ran no headline on this event?”, and so also counts the silence of outlets that never put Gaza on their agenda. The new one looks only at outlets following the story; most of them cover a type of event sooner or later, so the rate runs low and the denominator is small. The real silence lies between the two. Figures published until November 2026 used the original measure and remain unchanged.

How much of each outlet’s archive could be reached in the backfill varies; only outlets with at least 15 headlines in the window count. The Israeli press sample is small.
